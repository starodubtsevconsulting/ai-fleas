# Personal Governor calendar governance

Use calendar events as both human commitments and inspectable Governor context. The calendar is state/context; a scheduler or automation is the trigger when the Governor must actively return at a future time.

## Governor-created events

When the Personal Governor creates or materially restructures a meaningful event, include a private Governor section when useful. Keep it short enough to remain usable when the calendar is revisited.

Suggested structure:

```text
Governor
Primary purpose: ...
Before: ...
During: ...
After: ...
Evidence to bring back: ...
Trigger/follow-up: ...
```

Not every field is required. Do not add ceremony when the event is self-explanatory.

## Preserve the primary purpose

A meeting may be professional, social, educational, mentoring, family, recovery, or mixed. The Governor may identify at most a small number of low-friction secondary opportunities aligned with the human's goals, but must not distort the event's primary purpose or turn every relationship into an optimization exercise.

Examples of secondary opportunities include learning how another person uses AI, observing a recurring adoption problem, practicing communication, noticing reusable workflow knowledge, or maintaining a valuable relationship.

## Companion notes

When the Governor cannot or should not modify the original event—for example a shared/external calendar event—create a private side-by-side companion event/note on the governed human's calendar when the additional plan is valuable.

The companion should normally be transparent/free so it does not create a false additional busy commitment.

## Follow-up

If useful knowledge or an action is likely to exist only after the event, define an `After` instruction. Examples:

- ask the human what happened;
- extract observations and concrete evidence;
- identify reusable rules/workflow implications;
- update authorized memory;
- create an action only when justified;
- decide whether relationship follow-up is useful.

Do not create follow-up bureaucracy for ordinary events with no expected governance value.

## Trigger semantics

Calendar text does not itself initiate the Governor.

When the Governor should actively return to the human at a future time, use an authorized scheduler/automation trigger. The trigger should point back to the event/context and ask for the minimum useful follow-up.

Conceptually:

`event -> Governor instructions -> optional trigger -> human follow-up -> evidence -> memory/rules/actions`

## Revisit behavior

When reviewing the calendar, read available Governor instructions before reasoning about the event. Treat unfinished `Before`/`After` items as context, not automatic obligations: re-evaluate them against current goals, capacity, changed circumstances, and whether the event actually occurred.

## Authority

Calendar writes, companion events, reminders, and automations are external effects and remain subject to the Personal Governor's configured authority and human authorization rules.
