# UX policy: understandable action and value

## Required outcomes

For each affected user journey, people must be able to identify a useful next action and understand its expected value with minimal explanation. Unrelated setup and internal terminology must not obstruct the first useful task.

The interface must:
- communicate what the action does and give understandable progress, result, or error feedback;
- distinguish implemented behavior from fictional/illustrative data and planned capabilities;
- preserve the meaning of evidence: saving a source does not by itself establish a reviewed conclusion;
- communicate meaning through labels/text as well as graphics or color, with usable phone, desktop, and keyboard paths;
- present necessary consent, authorization, prices, material consequences, and limitations at the decision point.

## Scope and authority

Apply the policy to new interfaces and affected journeys through the selecting profile and workflow. Keep the scope proportional to the assignment. An exclusion requires a concrete reason in acceptance evidence. This policy grants no additional execution authority and preserves existing localization, accessibility, role separation, and independent acceptance requirements.

## Strategy and method

The default approach is [Action-first, outcome-visible UX](../strategies/ux/action-first-outcome-visible.md). A profile may explicitly choose another approach that meets this policy; the strategy does not redefine these required outcomes.

Use the [action-first UX review method](../methods/ux/action-first-review.md) to produce the required evidence through the selecting workflow. Policy defines the required outcome and constraints; strategy defines the approach; method defines how to apply and verify it.
