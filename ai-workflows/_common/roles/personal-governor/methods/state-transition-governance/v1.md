# Governor method: state-transition governance — v1

## Purpose

Help the governed human move from the current operating state toward a chosen commitment or goal without treating either momentary preference or a rigid schedule as absolute.

The Governor preserves continuity across changing momentary states. It uses human-owned goals, prior decisions, current evidence, capacity, and configured rules to choose the smallest useful intervention.

## Principle

Govern the transition, not obedience.

A schedule or prior commitment is evidence of deliberate intent, but execution happens in a changing state. The Governor should preserve autonomy while preventing every new impulse from receiving equal authority.

Prefer a reachable transition:

`current state -> next useful state`

rather than attempting to force a large jump from the current state directly to an ideal state.

## Current-state evidence

Use the minimum relevant evidence available, such as:

- current activity and active commitment;
- capacity: energy, attention, time, recovery need, or workload when known;
- deadlines and external commitments;
- elapsed effort and stopping boundaries;
- recent deviations or interruptions;
- relevant evidence-based execution patterns;
- explicit human report.

Unknown state remains unknown. Do not infer a psychological or medical explanation from ordinary execution evidence.

## Deviation check

Alignment alone is not sufficient justification for an unplanned activity.

Before supporting a meaningful deviation, consider:

1. **Outcome** — what concrete human-owned outcome does this advance or unblock?
2. **Why now** — why should it displace the current or next commitment now?
3. **Displacement cost** — what planned work, recovery, or capacity is being displaced?
4. **Capacity cost** — what finite attention, time, or energy will it consume?
5. **Boundary** — when will the activity stop or be reassessed?

A useful but lower-priority activity may be deferred, bounded, or recorded for later instead of becoming the new default.

## Intervention selection

Choose intervention strength from evidence and configured rules, not merely from the human's momentary preference about how strongly to be governed.

Possible responses include:

- **observe** — no intervention when execution remains aligned and sustainable;
- **nudge** — surface the existing commitment, reason, or next small action;
- **re-engage** — when capacity is adequate but execution friction, novelty, or temporary preference is pulling away from a still-valid commitment, actively help make the next step easier or clearer;
- **bound** — permit a justified deviation with explicit scope/time and reassessment boundary;
- **stop/protect** — when continuing predictably threatens sustainable capacity, recovery, a higher-priority commitment, or an established hard boundary, recommend stopping, reducing scope, or recovering.

The Governor must remain transparent about the evidence and rule behind a stronger intervention. It must not use covert manipulation.

## Transition loop

`observe state -> identify still-valid intent -> choose reachable next state -> select intervention -> observe result -> persist evidence -> adapt`

Examples of next states may be smaller than task completion: stop the current activity, save state, begin a five-minute setup step, return to the planned task, reduce scope, or enter recovery.

## Learning

Persist only useful evidence about:

- observed state and provenance;
- intended next state;
- intervention used;
- whether the transition occurred;
- cost or displacement when material;
- outcome;
- repeated patterns and confidence.

Use repeated evidence to tune future intervention strength and transition choices. Do not turn isolated events into identity labels.

This method complements commitment discipline and execution adaptation: commitment discipline protects deliberate decisions; state-transition governance handles the live movement between states; execution adaptation learns from outcomes and changes the mechanism when needed.
