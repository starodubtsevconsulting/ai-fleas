# Short experience + educational article

Use for a short public article that starts from something the author genuinely experienced and uses it to teach or expose one useful idea.

## Audience contract

- Broad professional reader; do not assume software-engineering knowledge unless the profile narrows the audience.
- Designed for people who may be curious but busy and unwilling to read a long essay.
- Target approximately a **2–3 minute read**. Prefer roughly 500–800 words when that is enough; shorter is welcome. Exceed the target only when the idea genuinely requires it.

## Composition

Inject these methods by default:

1. [`on-writing-well`](../methods/on-writing-well.md) — simple, uncluttered prose.
2. [`storyworthy`](../methods/storyworthy.md) — truthful personal experience and an element of change.
3. [`why-nobody-reads-your-shit`](../methods/why-nobody-reads-your-shit.md) — earn attention continuously and stop when the value is delivered.

Additional blogging guides may be combined when the editorial objective requires them, for example thought leadership or search-informed content.

## Shape

A useful default movement is:

`hook/moment -> what happened -> question or realization -> concise explanation -> why it matters to the reader -> small practical next step or resonant return`

This is not a mandatory heading structure. The finished article should read as one piece, not as a filled template.

## Constraints

- Firsthand experience is the narrative anchor, not proof that a general claim is universally true.
- Educational explanation should serve the story's central idea; avoid turning the middle into documentation.
- Prefer one central idea over comprehensive coverage.
- Avoid long prerequisite sections, framework inventories, architecture detail, or implementation detail unless the audience needs them.
- Preserve authentic uncertainty and limitations.
- Finish when the promised idea has landed.

## Acceptance check

A reader should be able to answer, after two or three minutes:

- What happened?
- What changed or was realized?
- What useful idea did I learn?
- Why might it matter to me?
- What could I try next?
