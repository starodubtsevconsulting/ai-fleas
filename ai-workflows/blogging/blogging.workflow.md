# Blogging workflow

Use this workflow for explicitly requested work that develops ideas, expertise, observations, or research into a
coherent body of articles. Preserve the author's voice, factual provenance, prior arguments, and editorial continuity.

## Purpose

Support recurring publication of useful articles while learning from published work and audience evidence. A single
article may be a writing task; this workflow governs the continuing editorial system around it.

## Composition

Compose an article from independent reusable layers rather than treating one writing style as universal:

1. **Template** — the kind of artifact being produced: its audience contract, approximate length, shape, and default
   method composition.
2. **Methods** — injectable writing techniques that can be combined across templates.
3. **Editorial guides** — objective-specific guidance such as thought leadership or search-informed content.
4. **Profile voice** — private author voice, audience, destination, and editorial conventions.

### Templates

- [short experience + educational article](templates/short-experience-educational.md) for a concise 2–3 minute public
  article that combines a real personal experience with one useful educational idea.
- [scripted Q&A / podcast-style article](templates/scripted-qa-podcast.md) for a concise fictional/framed interviewer
  dialogue that develops one idea through natural questions and answers.

### Methods

- [On Writing Well](methods/on-writing-well.md) for simple, uncluttered prose;
- [Storyworthy](methods/storyworthy.md) for truthful personal experience, narrative movement, and change;
- [Why Nobody Reads Your Shit](methods/why-nobody-reads-your-shit.md) for attention-first editing that continuously
  earns the reader's time.

### Editorial guides

- [thought leadership](guides/thought-leadership.md) for original, experience-backed perspective;
- [search-informed content](guides/search-informed-content.md) for real audience questions and demonstrated discovery
  demand.

Templates may inject methods by default, and a profile or explicit request may add/remove methods or guides. The
finished article should not expose the composition machinery unless doing so is itself useful to the reader.

The selected profile may define private voice guidance, audiences, destinations, and editorial conventions. A host may
supply research, publishing, and analytics mechanics, but this workflow does not select a CMS, provider, model,
transport, or platform agent.

## Required behavior

- Treat published articles, drafts, source material, and recorded human decisions as authoritative artifacts.
- Preserve source provenance and distinguish verified facts, attributed claims, interpretation, and opinion.
- Do not invent expertise, firsthand experience, audience response, quotations, or performance evidence.
- Preserve the author's recognizable voice and uncertainty; do not silently replace it with a generic house style.
- Check prior coverage before repeating a claim or topic, when that archive is available within the permitted scope.
- Require explicit authorization before publishing, distributing, or changing an already-published article.
- Store durable editorial observations only where the selected profile explicitly permits them.

## Typical flow

`idea or evidence -> select template -> inject methods/guides -> research -> draft -> factual and editorial review -> human approval -> publish -> observe evidence -> inform later work`

The flow is adaptive. Meaningful events include an idea appearing, research completing, a draft being reviewed,
publication, a factual correction, audience feedback, discovery evidence, and a justified follow-up opportunity.
