# Why Nobody Reads Your Shit

Reusable blogging method inspired by Steven Pressfield's *Nobody Wants to Read Your Sh*t*.

This is an interpretation of the book's broad attention-and-reader principle, not a reproduction of its text or structure.

## Intent

Assume the reader owes the writer no attention. Earn the next sentence, paragraph, and minute.

## Apply

- Make the piece about something the intended reader can care about, not merely something the author wants to explain.
- Make the title create a clear reason to look closer without using a misleading promise.
- Make the opening immediately useful, surprising, tense, specific, or human.
- Remove throat-clearing and background that delays the interesting part.
- Keep asking what the reader gets from continuing: curiosity, recognition, useful knowledge, surprise, or resolution.
- Prefer examples and consequences over abstract claims.
- Cut sections that are correct but do not advance the reader's understanding or interest.
- Do not confuse length, sophistication, or completeness with value.
- End before the article has exhausted its point.

## Review question

If the reader can leave at any sentence, what makes the next sentence worth reading?
