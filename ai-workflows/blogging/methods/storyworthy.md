# Storyworthy

Reusable blogging method inspired by Matthew Dicks's *Storyworthy*.

This is an interpretation of general storytelling principles, not a reproduction of the book.

## Intent

Use truthful personal experience to give an article movement, specificity, and change without requiring the whole article to be a conventional story.

## Apply

- Prefer a real moment the author experienced when one is relevant to the idea.
- Start close to something happening rather than explaining the entire context first.
- Give the human in the piece a want, problem, uncertainty, surprise, decision, or obstacle.
- Look for change: what was believed, expected, noticed, decided, or understood differently by the end?
- Use small concrete details that make the experience feel lived rather than manufactured.
- Let educational material interrupt or expand the story when useful, then return to the human thread.
- Never invent dialogue, emotion, chronology, conflict, or experience to make the story stronger.
- Do not force a full narrative arc when a small personal frame is enough.

## Review question

What changed, and can the reader feel that change through something that actually happened?
