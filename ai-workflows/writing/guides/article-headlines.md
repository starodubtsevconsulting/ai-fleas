# Article headlines

Use for human-facing articles across destinations. The title is a truthful promise to the intended reader, not a
summary of every conclusion or a trick to force a click.

## Default: clear premise, earned curiosity

- Name the subject or situation concretely enough that the title works without surrounding context.
- Offer one useful tension, surprising turn, question, or consequence that the article actually develops. It is fine
  to reveal a striking event; leave the explanation, implications, or method for the article.
- Prefer the author's natural voice over formulas, hype, generic benefit claims, or search-keyword stuffing.
- Use a subtitle when it adds context or reader value; do not repeat the title in different words.
- Make a few meaningfully different candidates after drafting, then test each against the article: Would the intended
  reader know what this is about? Is the promise accurate? Would the opening promptly begin to fulfill it? Does it
  still work in a feed or search result without the subtitle?
- For explanatory or search-led pieces, lead with the answer or reader problem when that is more useful than a
  narrative hook. For personal essays, a concrete incident may be the better entry point. Do not force one title
  shape onto every article.

Choose the strongest honest title and record a human decision when the final voice or claim materially changes.
