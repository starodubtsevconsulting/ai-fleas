# Article calibration contract

Use this contract during Writing intake before drafting. It defines what the article is trying to accomplish so Writer and Reviewer evaluate the same target. It is portable workflow policy: profiles and editorial projects may add private or project-specific strategy without weakening these fields.

## Required calibration

Record:

- **Purpose** — what should this article accomplish?
- **Target reader** — who specifically should benefit?
- **Reader problem** — what problem, question, tension, or interest brings that reader here?
- **Core claim** — what should the reader leave understanding, believing, or being able to do?
- **Evidence** — what real experience, source, experiment, or result substantiates the claim?
- **Tone** — the intended editorial mode, such as technical, exploratory, tutorial, retrospective, or opinion.
- **Desired reader action** — the proportionate next step after reading; no action is valid when none is warranted.
- **Success signal** — what observable outcome would suggest the article reached the intended reader or achieved its purpose?

## Workflow use

Writer preserves the effective calibration in the article brief and review handoff. Project/profile policy may add commercial objectives, strategic constraints, offers, or private success criteria.

Reviewer reads the exact revision once as the target reader, then checks whether the draft actually serves the calibration. A polished article aimed at the wrong reader or proving a different claim is a substantive finding even when its prose is strong.

Calibration is not publication authority, a factual source, or permission to invent evidence. If the article changes direction during drafting, update the calibration explicitly rather than silently rationalizing the finished draft.
